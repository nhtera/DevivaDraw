An infinite canvas is only as good as the device pointing at it. The component
adapts its chrome to the viewport and its pointer, and routes wheel, trackpad,
finger and stylus input by explicit policy rather than guesswork - all of it
built in, with one preference for the case a heuristic cannot settle.

## Layout tiers

The chrome has three tiers, picked from viewport size and the *primary
pointer*, and re-evaluated live - attach or unplug a mouse and the layout
follows:

| Tier | When | Chrome |
|---|---|---|
| **Phone** | Viewport narrower than 768px **or** shorter than 500px | Bottom toolbar and compact properties bar |
| **Tablet** | Desktop-sized viewport with a coarse primary pointer (an iPad in landscape) | Desktop layout at ≥44px touch targets: hamburger-only top bar, centered toolbar, undo/redo and zoom in a bottom-left island |
| **Desktop** | Fine pointer | Full-density desktop layout |

The height clause is what routes a *landscape phone* - wide enough to clear the
width breakpoint, but far too short for the desktop side panel - to the phone
chrome. An iPad with a trackpad attached reports a fine pointer and gets
desktop density, which is the right call: that pointer is precise.

On phones and tablets the chrome also clears the home indicator (safe-area
insets), sizes itself in `dvh` so it survives Safari's collapsing URL bar, pans
the camera to keep the caret visible when the on-screen keyboard opens, and
suppresses pull-to-refresh, double-tap zoom and tap flash over the canvas.

## Wheel and trackpad routing

A wheel event carries no reliable "I am a mouse" flag, and the two devices want
opposite defaults: a trackpad's two-finger scroll should pan, a mouse wheel's
notch should zoom. **Main menu → Preferences → Input device** settles it -
*Auto* (the default), *Trackpad*, or *Mouse wheel*, plus *Invert mouse zoom*.

| Mode | Plain wheel | Ctrl/Cmd + wheel | Shift + wheel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Auto** | Mouse notch zooms, trackpad scroll pans | Zoom at cursor | Pan horizontally |
| **Trackpad** | Pan | Zoom at cursor | Pan horizontally |
| **Mouse wheel** | Zoom at cursor (invertible) | Mouse notch pans vertically | Pan horizontally |

Two things hold in every mode: **a real trackpad pinch always zooms** (browsers
report it as a ctrl+wheel event, and only a provable wheel notch is ever allowed
to do something else), and the decision is made per event with no sticky state,
so nothing flaps between modes mid-gesture.

The preference is stored per device under `devivadraw:input-device:v1` and
applies live - no reload.

## Pen, finger and palm

**"Pen draws, finger pans"** (Preferences, opt-in): with a stylus and a drawing
tool active, a single-finger touch pans the camera instead of drawing. Taps with
the select tool still select, so nothing else about touch changes.

On regardless of that toggle, because they are correctness rather than
preference:

- A pen landing preempts a palm that touched down first.
- A resting palm cannot raise phantom hover after a stroke, or pop the context
  menu mid-stroke.

**Double-tap edits.** iOS Safari never synthesizes `dblclick` from a double-tap,
which once left table cells, shape labels, arrow labels and text uneditable by
touch. The touch layer recognizes double-taps itself and routes them through the
same edit dispatch as a desktop double-click, with a synchronous focus hand-off
so iOS raises the keyboard.

## Wiring your own chrome

Hosts that build their own UI on `@deviva-draw/engine` get the same policies as
pure, additive exports - `resolveWheelAction` and `WheelBehaviorPreference`, the
pipeline options `getWheelBehavior` and `getTouchDrawPolicy`, and
`shouldSuppressTouchLongPress` for a host touch layer. Wire them to your own
preference store, or leave them out and the engine behaves exactly as it did
before they existed. See [The Engine](/guides/engine/).
